Computational linguist

I’m a computational linguist and language technology researcher. I study what language models understand, and how formal theories of grammar, semantics and inference can be used to evaluate, constrain and extend them.

About

I’m a Visiting Scholar in language technology at the University of Helsinki, where I designed and teach the course Large Language Models and Generative AI for NLP. Alongside research, I lead large-model inference infrastructure and performance engineering at AMD Silo AI.

My training is in logic and formal grammar, at the London School of Economics and King’s College London. After over a decade in industry, I returned to research for my PhD in language technology at the University of Helsinki, completed with distinction in 2024. More under Industry experience.

Research

Formally grounded evaluation of LLMs
What benchmark scores really measure: shortcuts, memorisation or inference. My doctoral work showed that models keep much of their accuracy when the words a reader needs are removed, and recent work shows that the robustness of LLMs follows cues in the data, not prior exposure to the benchmark. I also work on uncertainty-aware inference, and co-organise the ELOQUENT lab at CLEF, including the 2027 Fragments task.
Formal semantics, grammar and prosody
Type-theoretic semantics and formal grammar. With Stergios Chatzikyriakidis I contribute to the Formal Atlas of formalised semantic theories (paper), and work on Path Grammars and the generative capacity of Dynamic Syntax. I also study how prosodic prominence connects to meaning, most recently in speech and language models.
Neuro-symbolic reasoning
Methods that address what diagnosis reveals: inference that can be checked by a proof or refuted by a countermodel, grammar-constrained decoding, and finding which linguistic phenomena Transformers need symbolic support for.
